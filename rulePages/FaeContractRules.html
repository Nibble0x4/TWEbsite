Fae Contracts
This is a page dedicated to explaining Fae Contracts! For most players, these are something to be wary of and careful about entering into. Such contracts can also be made by any players playing a Faerie race.

Be careful with these things. They can be quite tricky! ~ Nibble

Fae Contracts are exactly what they sound like, binding legal contracts written by a Fae and signed by a mortal.

There is a lot of thought that goes into Fae Contracts, and nobody (Fae or mortal) should enter into one flippantly. Below is a list of guidelines to assist you in the creation (or anticipation) of a contract.


Ground Rules
There are some basic rules that are true of all Fae Contracts. Let's cover those first:

  • Contracts are made under the observance and authority of the world of TWE itself, instead of by any legal group. This means that both parties involved are bound by the conditions of the contract as long as they are present in TWE and can expect immediate repercussions for breaching the terms enclosed therein. If one or both parties leave TWE for any length of time, unless it is provided for within the terms of the contract to be suspended, the contract is rendered void.
  • No contract can become binding unless both parties sign the contract with a physical implement under their own direct control while physically present, having read the full contents, being of uncompromised mind, of their own free will. A contract cannot be made that requires a party to sign another contract.
  • Fae contracts are always made between exactly two parties, the mortal and the fae. It is very rare for the Mortal Party to include more than 1 person, and the Fae Party can only be a single individual.
  • Sections are completed in order, but they can be revisited and revised at any point by any party prior to Finalization. No modifications can be made to any section after Finalization, with one single exception: The Mortal Party can add the True Name of the Fae Party to Section 1 after Finalization but before Enactment.



Section 1: Party Definition
This first section defines the mortal and fae parties. Information is given here to clearly set the identities of the individuals in each party. This information normally includes full name, lineage, nation of origin, or any other details to avoid ambiguity. It is rather short and simple in most cases, but can be worded strangely on occasion.

If any of the information in Section 1 does not match with those who sign the contract, then the contract has no power and is voided instantaneously.

The identification information of each party must be written into the contract by the OPPOSITE party.

The Fae Party must provide valid identification information, but is not required to disclose their True Name. It is in the best interest of the Mortal Party to learn and add the Fae Party's True Name for this section immediately following Finalization.


Section 2: Fae Incumbents
Section 2 covers all the conditions that the Fae Party are required to uphold.

This section typically manifests as a list of specifically worded and meticulously numbered Clauses.
These Clauses each specify one condition that the Fae Party must adhere to or else be held in violation of the Contract.
Each Clause must be something the Fae Party is capable of doing or can reasonably be expected to do within any timelines included within the Clause.
The magic of a Fae Contract grants additional powers to a Fae with regards to the Mortal Party, if such powers are provided for in the Contract. Such additional contract-granted powers are such:

  • Fae may act as Patrons. This involves them granting some power to the Mortal Party in exchange for something.
    • 'Power' here refers to spells, skills, or abilities gained by the Fae Party. It does not include ability scores.
    • Whatever power they give they must also be capable of on their own without the aid of a contract.
    • Whatever power they give is lost by the Fae Party until the conclusion of the contract.
    • 'Power' granted to the Mortal Party is level-based using a class of the Fae Party's choosing. The Mortal Party cannot use the power granted to them until their character level is high enough.
  • Fae may grant a "True Name" to the Mortal Party, one known only to them and the Fae Party. This can grant them some of the Fae traits that they lack.
  • Fae may perform 'Miracles', an expanded effect of some power already held by the Fae that can dramatically alter the course of the life of the Mortal Party. Some examples are as follows:
    • Give me a child: If the Fae has some sort of power related to life or manipulation of the body, they may be able to miraculously let a mortal conceive.
    • Save our Crops: If the Fae has some sort of power related to growth, the ground, and abundance, they may be able to offset a famine or bolster a harvest.
  • Fae Claiming as protection from other Fae. This marks the Mortal Party as belonging to the Fae Party in fae circles, meaning they cannot strike deals with, be named by, or be forced into games with other fae. Many fae tricks will fail against a claimed mortal.


The Fae Party must uphold and meet all the conditions of the Clauses in this section. Failure to do so will incur consequences as dictated in Section 3.


Section 3: Fae Repercussions
Section 3 covers the conseqeuences for the Fae Party should they fail to meet and uphold the conditions laid out in Section 2.

Typically, each numbered Clause has a listed repercussion should the Fae Party fail with regards to it. There is allowance for terms such as 'any' or 'all', each held to mean their logical definitions.
Some repercussions may include but are certainly not limited to:

  • Specific clauses or clause modifications to be added to or removed from specific sections of the contract.
  • The Voiding of the Contract as a whole (See section 8)
  • The forfeiture of listed items in Section 4 or the release and return of listed items in Section 7
  • The banishment of the Fae Party from That Which Exists



Section 4: Fae Collateral
Section 4 lists items that are given up by the Fae Party. These can be objects, powers, memories, creatures, whatever is possessed by the Fae Party.

These items are specified to either be:

  • Held: Given to the Mortal Party or some specified outside party, not to be recovered by the Fae Party until specified by some condition in another Section.
  • Entrusted: Given up and banished or locked away, somehow inaccessible to either party until specified by some condition in another Section.


The items in this list serve as additional encouragement for the Fae Party to uphold their portions of the Contract.


Section 5: Mortal Incumbents
Section 5 covers all the conditions that the Mortal Party is required to uphold.

This section typically manifests as a list of specifically worded and meticulously numbered Clauses.
These Clauses each specify one condition that the Mortal Party must adhere to or else be held in violation of the Contract.
Each Clause must be something the Mortal Party is capable of doing or can reasonably be expected be capable of doing within any timelines included within the Clause.

The Mortal Party must uphold and meet all the conditions of the Clauses in this section. Failure to do so will incur consequences as dictated in Section 6.


Section 6: Mortal Repercussions
Section 6 covers the conseqeuences for the Mortal Party should they fail to meet and uphold the conditions laid out in Section 5.

Typically, each numbered Clause has a listed repercussion should the Mortal Party fail with regards to it. There is allowance for terms such as 'any' or 'all', each held to mean their logical definitions.
Some repercussions may include but are certainly not limited to:

  • Specific clauses or clause modifications to be added to or removed from specific sections of the contract.
  • The Voiding of the Contract as a whole (See section 8)
  • The forfeiture of listed items in Section 7 or the release and return of listed items in Section 4



Section 7: Mortal Collateral
Section 7 lists items that are given up by the Mortal Party. These can be objects, powers, memories, creatures, whatever is possessed by the Mortal Party.

These items are specified to either be:

  • Held: Given to the Fae Party or some specified outside party, not to be recovered by the Mortal Party until specified by some condition in another Section.
  • Entrusted: Given up and banished or locked away, somehow inaccessible to either party until specified by some condition in another Section.


An option available to Mortals to offer as collateral is their Soul. This is a steep price to pay, but it is among the heaviest bartering chips possessed by mortals.
Fae may take the 'Soul' of the Mortal Party. They may hold it so long as the Contract specifies, and may do with it whatever is allowed in the Contract. Some effects of losing a soul are as follows:

  • Inability to cast Divine or Primal spells, and loss of Divine Blessings.
  • Reliance on Fae Party for the continued animation of their body.
  • Alignment is shifted to match that of the Fae holding the soul.
  • You are now considered Undead; you are harmed by Positive Energy and healed by Negative Energy.
  • Some benefits for the Fae Party gained by holding the soul of the Mortal Party are as follows:
    • Ability to destroy the soul, ending the life of the Mortal Party.
    • Ability to consume the soul, gaining power commensurate to the strength of the soul (As little as some bonus HP, as much as potentially multiple levels worth of power) but destroying the soul.
    • Ability to alter the physical form of the Mortal Party, marking them as your own and twisting certain aspects such as age, appearance, race, and actions.


The items in the above list serve as additional encouragement for the Mortal Party to uphold their portions of the Contract.


Section 8: Conditions of Void
Section 8 lists each of the ways that the Contract can be rendered Void.

A Void Contract enacts a few final Clauses before becoming powerless. The exact Clauses enacted depend on the manner by which the Contract becomes Void.
Once a Contract becomes powerless, both parties are released from all Clauses in all other sections not covered by Clauses in Section 8.
Any collateral not provided for in the applied Clauses of Void are released and returned, and any powers granted through the magic of the Fae Contract are returned or lost.


Finalization
After the 8 Sections are written, they are reviewed and are open to discussion or revision. During this time, both parties are free to make changes to previous Sections as long as the other party is aware of the change.

After all changes are made, the both parties must agree to enter Finalization. Once both parties agree to Finalization, the Mortal Party signs the contract first, signing the same name written by the Fae Party at the beginning of the process
During the Mortal Party's signing, they may write the True Name of the Fae Party into Section 1, overwriting the name they provided before. If they do, then they may add one more Clause (with the DM's approval) to Section 3 or remove a Clause from Sections 5 or 6.
Once the Mortal Party ahs completed their signing, the Fae Party MUST sign as well.


Enactment
With all sections finalized, the Contract is enacted and exerts its power.
